# Research plan — five workstreams

Run these in parallel via subagents (Agent / general-purpose) when available; work
through them inline otherwise. Research must finish before you build the PDF.
Agents commonly exhaust web-search budgets — that is expected. Capture what they
found, and tag every gap **VERIFY**. State absence-of-evidence findings plainly
(e.g. "no public listing found anywhere" is a real, useful result).

Give every agent the same standing instruction: **a source URL for every claim,
exact quotes for the important ones, flag contradictions and undated claims, and
never invent figures — say "not found" instead.**

## 1 — Sale status & seller

> Research whether <ASSET> at <LOCATION> is actually for sale, and everything
> about the sale. Search in the local language and English: listings, brokers,
> MLS/portals, auction/foreclosure/insolvency notices, local news, and social.
> Report: whether a listing exists (with URL), asking price and what it includes,
> broker/contact, any distress or reason-for-sale signals, dates, and — if you
> find nothing — say so explicitly and list what you searched. Also profile the
> owner: background, why they bought, tenure, any prior sale/capital-raise
> attempts, corporate entity name.

## 2 — Physical asset inventory

> Build the most detailed possible physical asset inventory of <ASSET>. Cover:
> land area and tenure, forest/pasture split and any conservation regime; every
> building and structure; accommodation (room types, exact key count, capacity,
> published rates over time); energy (generation type and capacity, grid status);
> water (sources, rights basis); waste/biogas; agriculture and livestock with
> numbers; value-add production; amenities; access and logistics (distances,
> road condition); staff and any volunteer programme; certifications. A source
> for every number; flag where sources disagree and which figures are dated.

## 3 — Operating history, revenue model & reputation

> Reconstruct the operating history and commercial model of <ASSET>: founding and
> timeline, closures/reopenings, management changes; current and historical
> pricing by product with dates; all revenue lines and whether each has a
> published price and a bookable path; demand signals (ratings and review counts
> over time, seasonality, guest profile, sell-out evidence); the review-sentiment
> trajectory across the years, quoting representative reviews with dates;
> certifications, awards, and press; staffing over time. Flag contradictions and
> undated claims; say "not found" rather than estimating.

## 4 — Market context & comparables

> Research the market and valuation comparables for acquiring <ASSET-TYPE> in
> <REGION>. Provide: the tourism/market backdrop (arrivals, occupancy, ADR,
> RevPAR, spend, seasonality, source markets, currency/competitiveness trends
> with dates and sources); the competitive set with key counts and rates,
> distinguishing sub-markets; comparable transactions AND asking prices (name,
> size in ha, keys, price, $/key and $/ha computed, source, date — at least 10);
> land-value gradient (raw vs. development vs. serviced); yield/operating
> benchmarks (occupancy, RevPAR, GOP margins, EBITDA multiples, cap rates); and
> demand tailwinds (wellness/regenerative growth, PSA/carbon rates, tax
> incentives). Use tables. Flag estimates vs. reported figures.

## 5 — Risk, legal & regulatory

> Research the risk and regulatory picture for acquiring <ASSET> in <JURISDICTION>.
> Keep general law high-level but be specific on what changes the deal. Cover:
> property-specific litigation/liens/tax/permit issues; environmental and
> setback rules (riparian/lake buffers, forest law, protected areas) and any
> recent legal changes; water and operating-permit requirements; foreign-ownership
> and transaction mechanics (share vs. asset, transfer/stamp/capital-gains/
> property tax rates, labour/severance liability on transfer); and natural
> hazards (seismic, volcanic, flood, landslide, access-road fragility). Separate
> "confirmed", "likely", and "must verify with local counsel". Do not invent
> legal provisions or rates.

## Synthesis

Merge the five dossiers, deduplicating and reconciling. Where two workstreams
disagree on a figure, that is a **FLAG**, not a silent averaging. Carry the TERRA
reading (workstream-independent, first-party) as the anchor for anything physical
about the land. Keep the raw dossiers as working files so figures can be
re-checked when writing the report.
