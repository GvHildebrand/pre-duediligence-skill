# Report structure — the 16-section pre-DD template

This is the spine of the deliverable. Not every asset needs every sub-point, but
the section order and intent should hold. The aim is a document a buy-side
principal can act on and hand to counsel.

## Front matter

**Cover** — asset name, "Preliminary (pre-acquisition) due-diligence report",
one-line asset descriptor, the TERRA land score badge (if run), prepared-for /
purpose / date / status / classification block. Status line should always say
this is preliminary and that confirmatory DD has not been performed.

**Document control & how to read this** — one paragraph on what a pre-DD report
is and is not, the confidence-tag key, a contents table, and — critically — a
callout box surfacing the 1–3 findings that change the diligence plan immediately
(usually the TERRA flags). Put the flags on page 1; do not make the reader hunt.

## The sixteen sections

1. **Executive summary** — the thesis in a paragraph, a 4-stat strip, "what we
   can stand behind", "what must be resolved before a number is committed", and a
   boxed recommendation with an anchor price and the express conditions. This is
   the only page some readers read; make it self-contained.

2. **Asset identification & title screen** — legal name, entity, centroid,
   title area (reconcile conflicting acreage figures), jurisdiction, number of
   parcels, foreign-ownership position, encumbrance watch-list. Flag boundary and
   jurisdiction ambiguities here.

3. **TERRA land-intelligence reading** — the first-party section. Scope note
   (1 km² box vs. titled boundary), the score + model fit, headline readings and
   physical metrics in two tables, the embedded figures (score/radar, satellite,
   analytical layers), and a "what it means for the deal" table translating each
   reading into a DD implication. End with TERRA's capital-deployment / best-fit
   verdict. See `terra-workflow.md`.

4. **Physical asset inventory** — land & environmental, accommodation (distinguish
   sellable premium keys from dorm/other), infrastructure & production, and
   intangibles (certifications, brand, staff, channels). Note explicitly what is
   NOT confirmed to exist, so nobody underwrites a phantom spa.

5. **Commercial & operating snapshot** — business model and any shifts (e.g.
   unbundling), ratings, demand trend, unbuilt revenue lines, working-capital
   features, condition trajectory. This is where you read the review corpus for
   what's really happening.

6. **Market context & demand** — arrivals/occupancy/ADR/spend at national and
   sub-market level, the currency/timing backdrop, and the crucial sub-market
   distinction (e.g. dirt-road village vs. paved luxury corridor). Include the
   proof-point comp that shows the ceiling is reachable.

7. **Industry benchmarks** — transaction & pricing (per key, per ha, EBITDA
   multiples, the anchor trade), operating (ADR/RevPAR/occupancy, cap-rate
   proxies), and the TERRA land-quality percentile table. Draw from
   `benchmark-library.md`; re-date everything.

8. **Transaction structure & tax** — share vs. asset trade-offs, indicative
   closing costs and tax rates, and a recommended structure. Keep legal depth to
   what the user asked for.

9. **Preliminary valuation** — five explicit triangulation methods (raw-land
   floor, rural-land upper, per-key, improved $/ha, development-land, income
   multiple). REJECT the ones that don't hold and say why. Conclude with an
   anchor range and the conditions that move it. Always note capex must be
   underwritten with the price as one number.

10. **Legal & regulatory red-flag screen** — a table of items, each a
    counsel workstream, each tagged. High-level unless the user asked for depth.
    Include a short "not the risk you may be told" note to kill false alarms.

11. **Consolidated risk matrix** — risk × likelihood × impact × mitigation/DD
    action. Rank by how much each could change the deal.

12. **Confirmatory DD workplan** — the standard buy-side workstreams (title,
    environmental, utilities/concessions, HR/labour, financial, commercial,
    technical/physical, legal/corporate, tax) with scope and tier (1 = before
    spending real money; 2 = before signing; 3 = structuring).

13. **Information (data-room) request list** — the concrete documents to ask the
    seller for, grouped (corporate & title, financial, operations & people,
    assets/permits/environmental). This should be sendable as-is.

14. **Indicative process & timeline** — phases from "confirm the sale is real"
    through close, with indicative weeks.

15. **Questions for the owner** — 4–6 questions for the first substantive
    meeting, each of which could end the process cheaply. Frame them to be asked
    as a steward, not an interrogator.

16. **Sources, method & disclaimer** — method paragraph (what data, what access
    you did and didn't have), principal sources, and a firm standing/disclaimer:
    not a valuation, appraisal, audit, or legal/tax/investment advice; everything
    tagged VERIFY or FLAG is unresolved by design; confirm with local counsel.

## Tone & formatting discipline

- Institutional but human. Prose and paragraphs carry the argument; tables carry
  comparisons. Avoid bullet-spam.
- Lead with the answer. Executive summary and each section open with the
  conclusion, then support it.
- Show your work on valuation and reject weak methods explicitly — that candour
  is what makes the number credible.
- Keep flags visible: exec summary + document-control callout + inline tags.
- Neutral on the seller. The community/steward framing matters when the seller
  is a founder; a buyer who leads with stewardship often beats one who leads with
  price.
